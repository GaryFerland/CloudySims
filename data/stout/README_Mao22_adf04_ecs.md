## Mao et al. (2022): STOUT Conversion and Atomic Data Comparison

We converted the H-like and He-like atomic data from Mao et al. (2022, `2022ApJS..263...35M`) from ADAS ADF04 format into Cloudy's STOUT format. The conversion includes 50 ions (25 H-like and 25 He-like), generating `.nrg`, `.tp`, and `.coll` files containing energy levels, radiative transition probabilities (Einstein A-values), and effective electron collision strengths, respectively. The current conversion uses the **`adf04_ecs`** directory of the original Mao+22 archive, preserving the original level indices and numerical data. The datasets contain 36 levels for H-like ions and 71 levels for He-like ions, with explicitly resolved levels extending through \(n=6\). No higher-\(n\) averaged shells were identified.

We validated the conversion against the original ADF04 files for all 50 ions and performed additional independent comparisons with Mao+22's separately tabulated data for selected ions. We also adjusted the output formatting to match Cloudy's existing STOUT file conventions. These checks confirmed that the converted files reproduce the selected source data. However, successful conversion does not necessarily establish that the selected atomic data are the most accurate available.

We subsequently compared the converted Fe XXV and Fe XXVI datasets with CHIANTI. The energy levels showed relatively small differences, while some radiative A-values exhibited substantial discrepancies. The most significant discrepancy identified was for the Fe XXV electric-quadrupole transition \(1s6d\,{}^1D_2 \rightarrow 1s^2\,{}^1S_0\), for which the Mao+22 excitation data give \(A=4.98\times10^{11}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\), compared with \(3.58\times10^{10}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\) in CHIANTI, a relative difference of approximately **1,291%**. Examination of the \(1snd\,{}^1D_2\) to ground-state transition series showed that the discrepancies increase substantially from \(n=3\) to \(n=6\).

To investigate the origin of this difference, we examined the alternative atomic-structure data provided in Mao+22's **`adf04_str`** directory. For the same Fe XXV transition, `adf04_str` gives \(A=2.38\times10^{10}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\), approximately 21 times smaller than the value in `adf04_ecs` and considerably closer to CHIANTI. Similar differences were found for other transitions in the investigated series. Importantly, the original `adf04_ecs` file contains exactly the large A-value reproduced by our converter, demonstrating that the discrepancy was **not introduced during the conversion**. Instead, the original Mao+22 archive contains different sets of radiative transition probabilities.

Mao et al. discuss different AUTOSTRUCTURE calculations, including AS-RM and AS-REL, which may explain the differences between the excitation and structure datasets. However, the precise correspondence between these calculations and the individual archive directories still requires clarification. We are contacting the authors to determine which energy levels and radiative A-values should be combined with the R-matrix collision strengths for astrophysical modeling. **For now, the STOUT files in this branch use `adf04_ecs` exclusively.** They represent a validated conversion of that dataset, while the final selection of atomic data for Cloudy remains under investigation.
